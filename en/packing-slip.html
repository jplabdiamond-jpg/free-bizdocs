A packing slip (US) or delivery note (UK / Australia) travels with the goods and lists exactly what was shipped. It does not show prices - that information belongs on the invoice. The recipient checks the contents against the slip and signs it as proof of delivery.

What to include on a packing slip

  • Your business details and a packing-slip or delivery-note number
  • The customer’s billing details and the ship-to address
  • The PO or order number so the shipment can be matched to the order
  • Each item with the quantity shipped
  • Ship date and a received-by / signature line

Packing slip vs invoice

The packing slip confirms what was physically delivered; the invoice requests payment. Create both from the same details - enter the items once, then switch documents under the preview.

Frequently asked questions

What should a packing slip include?

Your business details, a packing-slip number, the customer’s billing details and the ship-to address, the PO or order number, each item with the quantity shipped, the ship date, and a received-by/signature line. Prices are left off.

Does a packing slip need to be signed?

Not legally, but a signature (or received-by line) is common so the recipient can confirm what arrived — useful as proof of delivery if there’s a dispute.

Should a packing slip show prices?

Usually not. It lists items and quantities only; prices go on the invoice. This tool hides the price and tax columns on packing slips automatically.

What is the difference between a packing slip and a delivery note?

They serve the same purpose. “Packing slip” is the common term in the US and Canada, “delivery note” in the UK and Australia - the title changes with the country you select.
