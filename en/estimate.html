An estimate gives a customer an approximate price before work starts; a quote (or quotation) is a fixed price valid for a set period. Both use the same layout - this tool names the document to match your region (“Estimate” in the US and Canada, “Quotation” in the UK, “Quote” in Australia).

What a good estimate or quote includes

  • Your business details and the customer’s name (“Prepared For”)
  • A number, the date and a “valid until” date
  • Itemised scope of work with quantities and unit prices
  • Tax shown separately so the customer sees the final total
  • Terms (deposit, exclusions, payment schedule) and an acceptance signature line

Estimate vs quote vs proposal

Most small businesses use the terms interchangeably, but a quote is generally treated as a firm price the customer can accept, while an estimate is a best guess that may change. A proposal adds the approach, timeline and qualifications. Whatever you call it, state clearly what is included, what is not, and how long the price is valid.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an estimate and a quote?

An estimate is an approximate price that may change; a quote (or quotation) is a fixed price the customer can accept, valid for a set period. Say clearly which one you’re issuing and include a “valid until” date.

How long should a quote be valid?

Set a “valid until” date so prices don’t stay open indefinitely — 14 to 30 days is common. After it expires you can re-issue with updated pricing.

Is a quote legally binding?

Rules vary by country and contract, but a quote accepted by the customer is generally treated as an agreed price for the stated scope and validity period. An estimate is usually not binding. Say which one you are issuing and include your terms.

Can I convert the estimate to an invoice?

Yes. Under the preview, click “Invoice” - the line items, customer and your details are carried over and a new invoice number is assigned.

Does the PDF include tax?

Yes. Tax is calculated for the selected country (sales tax, VAT, GST or GST/HST) and shown separately from the subtotal.
