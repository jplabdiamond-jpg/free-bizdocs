An expense report (or expense claim) is how an employee or contractor asks to be reimbursed for business costs they paid out of pocket. It lists each expense by date and category with the amount, totals the reimbursement due, and leaves a line for an approver to sign off.

What to include on an expense report

  • Who is claiming, and who it is submitted to (the employer or client)
  • The period the expenses cover and a report number
  • One line per expense: the date, category and description, and the amount
  • The total reimbursement due
  • An approval line — and keep the receipts to attach

Tips for a claim that gets approved

Group expenses by category (travel, meals, supplies), put the date and merchant in the description, and keep a receipt for every line. Totals should match the receipts exactly. This tool keeps the layout simple — description and amount — so the claim is quick to scan and sign.

Frequently asked questions

What should an expense report include?

Who is claiming and who it is submitted to, the period covered, one line per expense (date, category, description, amount), the total reimbursement due, and an approval line. Keep a receipt for each line.

What is the difference between an expense report and an expense claim?

They are the same thing — “expense report” is common in the US and Canada, “expense claim” in the UK and Australia. The title switches to match the country you select.

Does the expense report calculate tax?

No. It simply totals the amounts you enter for reimbursement. Enter each expense as its final paid amount.

Do I need to attach receipts?

Usually yes — most employers require a receipt for each expense. Submit the PDF with the receipts attached so the claim can be verified and approved.
