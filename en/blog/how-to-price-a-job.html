How to Price a Job: Hourly, Fixed and Value-Based Pricing

Quoting · Free BizDoc · Nov 5, 2026

Pricing too low is the quickest way to stay busy and broke. A solid price starts from your real costs and target income, then uses the method that fits the job. Here is how to work it out.

Start from your real costs

Add up what the work truly costs you: your time, materials, tools, software, travel, and a share of overheads like insurance and admin. Then add the profit margin you need to grow. If a price does not cover all of this, it is a loss dressed up as a sale.

Set a target hourly rate

Even for fixed pricing, know your hourly rate. Take the annual income you want, add costs, and divide by the billable hours you can realistically work (not all 2,000+ in a year). That floor stops you underquoting.

Choose a pricing method

  • Hourly — best when scope is uncertain; you bill for time spent.
  • Fixed price — best when scope is clear; the client gets certainty and you keep the upside if you work efficiently.
  • Value-based — price on the outcome’s worth to the client, not your hours; powerful when the result drives real revenue for them.

Quote with confidence

Present one clear price (or a small good/better/best set), state what it includes, and avoid apologising for it. If a client pushes back, adjust scope, not just the number — drop a deliverable rather than your rate.

Frequently asked questions

Should I charge hourly or a fixed price?

Charge hourly when the scope is unclear and could expand; charge a fixed price when the scope is well defined. Fixed pricing rewards efficiency and gives the client certainty, but only quote it once you understand the work.

How do I work out my hourly rate?

Take the yearly income you want, add your business costs, and divide by the hours you can realistically bill in a year. Remember that not every working hour is billable, so the divisor is lower than you think.

A client says my price is too high &mdash; what do I do?

Adjust the scope rather than simply cutting your rate. Remove a deliverable or reduce the quantity so the lower price still reflects the value and keeps you profitable.
