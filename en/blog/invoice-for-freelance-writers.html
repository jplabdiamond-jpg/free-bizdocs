How to Invoice as a Freelance Writer

Industries · Free BizDoc · Oct 26, 2026

Freelance writers bill in a few ways — per word, per piece, per hour, or on a monthly retainer — and the invoice is where you also pin down kill fees and rights. A clean invoice gets you paid faster and keeps the relationship clear.

Pick a model and show it clearly

Whether you charge per word, per article or a project fee, show the calculation plainly: “Feature article, 1,500 words” with the total, or “Content retainer — October (4 posts)”. For hourly work, show hours and rate.

Kill fees and rights

If a commissioned piece is cancelled, a kill fee (an agreed portion of the fee) may apply — note it in your terms. State what rights you’re granting (first use, full transfer, etc.) if that affects the price.

Withholding and tax

In some countries, payments to individual writers are subject to withholding, and the payer deducts tax before paying you — your invoice should reflect the arrangement. Add VAT/GST only if you’re registered; US sales tax rarely applies to writing services.

Frequently asked questions

How do freelance writers charge?

Common models are per word, per article/project, per hour, or a monthly retainer. Show the basis clearly on the invoice (e.g. word count and total, or the retainer period and deliverables).

What is a kill fee?

A kill fee is an agreed portion of the fee paid if a commissioned piece is cancelled before publication. Note it in your terms so it’s clear what’s owed if a project is pulled.

Do writers charge tax on invoices?

Usually only VAT/GST if registered; US sales tax rarely applies to writing services. Be aware some countries require the payer to withhold tax from payments to individuals.
