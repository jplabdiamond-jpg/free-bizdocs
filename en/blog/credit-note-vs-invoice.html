What Is a Credit Note? (Credit Note vs Invoice)

Invoicing · Free BizDoc · Oct 23, 2026
In short

An invoice asks a customer to pay; a credit note does the opposite - it cancels or reduces a previously issued invoice and reverses the related tax.

A credit note (or credit memo) is a document that reduces or cancels an amount a customer owes on a previous invoice — for a return, an overcharge, a discount or a correction. It’s the proper way to fix an invoice without deleting it.

When to issue a credit note

  • A customer returns goods or cancels part of an order.
  • You overcharged, or applied the wrong price or tax.
  • You agree a discount or goodwill adjustment after invoicing.

Credit note vs invoice vs refund

An invoice adds to what a customer owes; a credit note reduces it. A credit note isn’t the same as a refund — it adjusts the balance, which the customer can set against what they owe, while a refund actually returns money. You can issue a credit note and then refund if appropriate.

What to include

Reference the original invoice number, show the items and amounts being credited (including tax), and give the credit note its own unique number. Keep both documents on file so your records reconcile.

Frequently asked questions

What is a credit note used for?

To reduce or cancel an amount on a previous invoice — for returns, overcharges, discounts or corrections. It’s the proper way to adjust an invoice without deleting it.

Is a credit note the same as a refund?

No. A credit note reduces the balance the customer owes; a refund returns money. You can issue a credit note and then refund if the customer has already paid.

What should a credit note include?

Its own unique number, a reference to the original invoice, the items and amounts being credited (with tax), and your business and customer details.
