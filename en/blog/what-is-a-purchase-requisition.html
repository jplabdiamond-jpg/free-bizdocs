What Is a Purchase Requisition? (vs a Purchase Order)

Purchasing · Free BizDoc · Nov 25, 2026

A purchase requisition is the internal request to buy something — raised and approved inside a company before any order reaches a supplier. It’s the step before the PO. Here is how it works.

What it is

A purchase requisition is a form an employee raises to say “we need to buy this” and to get internal approval for it. It stays inside the company — it goes to a manager or purchasing team, not to the supplier. Only once it’s approved does a purchase order get created.

Requisition vs purchase order

  • Requisition — internal; asks permission to buy; audience is your own approvers
  • Purchase order — external; places the order; audience is the supplier

The requisition authorises; the PO orders. One never leaves the building, the other does.

Why companies use them

Requisitions put a control before the spend: budget and need are checked before any commitment to a supplier exists. They create an audit trail of who requested what and who approved it, which is why larger organisations route purchases through requisition → approval → PO.

Do small businesses need them?

For a sole trader or tiny team, a formal requisition is usually overkill — you decide and raise the PO yourself. They earn their keep once several people can spend and someone needs to approve before money is committed. The purchase order, by contrast, is useful even for a one-person business.

Frequently asked questions

What is a purchase requisition?

An internal request an employee raises to get approval to buy something. It stays inside the company and goes to an approver, not the supplier. Once approved, a purchase order is created.

What's the difference between a requisition and a purchase order?

A requisition is internal and asks permission to buy; a purchase order is external and places the order with the supplier. The requisition authorises the spend, the PO orders the goods.
