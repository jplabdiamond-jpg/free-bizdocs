What to Include in a Quote: A Simple Checklist

Quoting · Free BizDoc · Nov 7, 2026

A good quote does two jobs: it wins the work and it prevents arguments later. Both come from being specific. Here is the checklist every quote should cover.

The essentials

  • Your business name, contact details and (if registered) tax number
  • The client’s name and the date
  • A unique quote number
  • An itemised list of the work with prices
  • Subtotal, tax, and a clear total

Scope: say what is and is not included

The biggest source of disputes is scope. Spell out exactly what the price covers, and add a short “not included” line for the obvious extras (materials, revisions beyond a number, travel). Being explicit protects both sides.

Validity and terms

  • Valid until a date — prices and availability change, so cap how long the quote holds.
  • Payment terms (deposit, due date, method).
  • Any assumptions the price depends on.
  • How changes to scope will be priced.

Make it easy to accept

End with a simple way to say yes — a line to sign, a reply to confirm, or an accept button. The faster a client can accept, the more quotes turn into jobs. When they accept, convert the quote into an invoice with the same numbers.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a quote be valid?

Commonly 14 to 30 days. Put a 'valid until' date on every quote so you are not held to old prices or availability if the client comes back months later.

What is the difference between a quote and an estimate?

A quote is a fixed price you commit to; an estimate is an informed approximation that may change. If you label it a quote, be ready to honour the figure.

Should a quote show tax?

If you are registered for VAT/GST or charge sales tax, show the tax and a clear total so the client knows the final amount. If you are not registered, there is no tax line.
