How to Ask for a Deposit (Deposit Invoices)

Getting paid · Free BizDoc · Oct 21, 2026

Asking for a deposit protects you from doing work that never gets paid for, and it filters out clients who were never serious. Done professionally, it’s completely normal — here’s how to ask and how to invoice it.

Why take a deposit

A deposit commits the client, covers your early costs, and improves cash flow on longer jobs. For new clients, larger projects, or custom work you can’t resell, it’s standard practice.

How much, and how to ask

Common deposits run 25–50% of the total. Set it on the quote so it’s agreed before work starts, and phrase it as routine: “To book you in, I take a 50% deposit, with the balance due on completion.” Confidence matters more than apology.

Invoicing the deposit and the balance

Issue a deposit invoice for the agreed amount up front. On the final invoice, show the full total, then the deposit already paid as a deduction, leaving the balance due. This keeps your records clean and the maths obvious to the client.

Frequently asked questions

How much deposit should I ask for?

Commonly 25–50% of the total, depending on the work and your costs. Set it on the quote so it’s agreed before you start.

How do I invoice a deposit?

Issue a deposit invoice for the agreed amount up front. On the final invoice, show the full total, subtract the deposit already paid, and bill the remaining balance.

Is it normal to ask clients for a deposit?

Yes — for new clients, larger projects and custom work it’s standard. Phrase it as routine policy and most clients expect it.
