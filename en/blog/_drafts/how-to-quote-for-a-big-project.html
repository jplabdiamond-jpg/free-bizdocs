How to Quote for a Big Project (Without Underpricing)

Quoting · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Big projects are where quotes go badly wrong — one number for months of work hides all the risk. The fix is to break it down. Here is how to quote a large project without underpricing yourself.

Break it into parts

Don’t quote a big project as a single figure you guessed. Decompose it into phases or deliverables, estimate each, and build the total from the parts. A breakdown is more accurate, easier for the client to understand, and far easier to adjust if the scope shifts.

Build in contingency

Long projects have unknowns, and unknowns cost time. Add a sensible contingency for the parts you’re least sure about, rather than quoting the rosy best case. Underpricing a big job doesn’t win it on good terms — it wins you months of resented, unpaid overrun.

Structure the payment

Never carry a big project to the end unpaid. Structure it as a deposit up front and milestone payments tied to phases, so cash flows as the work does and your exposure stays limited. Put the payment schedule in the quote so it’s agreed from the start.

Protect the scope

On a long job, scope creep is the biggest risk to your margin. State clearly what’s included, what isn’t, and how changes are handled (quoted separately). Set an expiry on the quote too — your costs over a multi-month project can move, and you’ll want to reprice if it’s accepted much later.

Frequently asked questions

How do I quote for a large project?

Break it into phases or deliverables, price each part, and build the total from the breakdown rather than guessing one figure. Add contingency for the uncertain parts and structure payment as a deposit plus milestones.

Should I add contingency to a big quote?

Yes. Long projects have unknowns that cost time. Add a sensible contingency for the parts you're least sure about instead of quoting the best case, which leads to unpaid overrun.

How should payment work on a big project?

Take a deposit up front and tie further payments to milestones or phases, so cash flows as the work progresses and your exposure stays limited. Put the schedule in the quote.
