UK VAT Invoice Requirements: What to Include

Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

If you’re VAT-registered in the UK, your invoices must meet HMRC’s requirements so your customers can reclaim VAT and your records stand up to scrutiny. Here’s a plain-English checklist of what a full VAT invoice should show.

What a full VAT invoice must include

  • A unique, sequential invoice number.
  • Your business name, address and VAT registration number.
  • The invoice date (and the “time of supply”/tax point if different).
  • The customer’s name and address.
  • A description of the goods or services.
  • For each line: quantity, unit price (excl. VAT), the VAT rate and the amount.
  • The total excluding VAT, the total VAT, and the amount including VAT — in GBP.

UK VAT rates

The standard rate is 20%, with a reduced rate of 5% for some goods and services and a 0% rate for certain zero-rated items. Show the rate that applies to each line, and if you supply items at different rates, show the VAT for each rate separately.

Simplified and modified invoices

For smaller retail sales (broadly under £250 including VAT) you can issue a simplified VAT invoice with fewer details. Larger or B2B sales usually need a full VAT invoice as above.

This is general guidance, not tax advice. VAT rules and thresholds change — check the current requirements on GOV.UK or with your accountant.

Frequently asked questions

What must a UK VAT invoice include?

A unique invoice number, your name, address and VAT registration number, the date, the customer’s details, a description, and for each line the quantity, net price, VAT rate and VAT amount, plus the totals excluding and including VAT in GBP.

Do I need to show my VAT number on invoices?

Yes. If you’re VAT-registered, a valid VAT invoice must show your VAT registration number so your customer can reclaim the VAT.

What are the UK VAT rates?

The standard rate is 20%, with a 5% reduced rate for some supplies and a 0% zero rate for certain items. Show the rate that applies to each line.
