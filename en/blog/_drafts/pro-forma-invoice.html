What Is a Pro Forma Invoice? (And When to Use One)

Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

A pro forma invoice is a preliminary bill you send before the sale is finalised — a good-faith estimate of what the customer will owe, used to confirm details or arrange payment in advance. It looks like an invoice but isn’t a demand for payment or an accounting record.

Pro forma invoice vs regular invoice

A regular (commercial) invoice is issued once goods or services are delivered and is a formal request for payment that goes into your books. A pro forma is issued beforehand, isn’t recorded as a sale, and can still change. Many businesses mark it clearly with the words “Pro Forma” so there’s no confusion.

When to use one

  • To give a buyer a firm price and details so they can approve the purchase or raise a PO.
  • To request an advance payment or deposit before starting work.
  • For international shipments, where customs may need a pro forma for valuation.
  • When a customer’s finance team needs a document to set up payment before the real invoice.

What to include

Use the same layout as an invoice — your details, the customer, line items, the expected total and any tax — but title it “Pro Forma Invoice” and add a validity date. When the sale is confirmed, issue a normal invoice with an invoice number for your records.

Frequently asked questions

What is the purpose of a pro forma invoice?

To give a buyer a preliminary, good-faith bill before the sale is final — to confirm price and details, request an advance, or support customs valuation. It is not a formal demand for payment or an accounting record.

Is a pro forma invoice legally binding?

Generally no. It is a quotation-like document that can still change. The binding request for payment is the final commercial invoice issued once the sale is confirmed.

What is the difference between a pro forma and a regular invoice?

A pro forma is sent before delivery and isn’t recorded as a sale; a regular invoice is sent after delivery, carries an invoice number, and is a formal request for payment in your books.
