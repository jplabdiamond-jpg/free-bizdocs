Milestone Billing: How to Invoice a Project in Stages

Getting paid · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

On a long project, invoicing only at the end means carrying all the risk and all the cost yourself for months. Milestone billing splits the project into stages you bill as you go. Here is how it works.

What milestone billing is

Instead of one invoice at the end, you divide the project into milestones — defined points like “design approved”, “first draft delivered”, “launch” — and invoice a portion of the fee as each is reached. It’s standard for larger or longer engagements.

How to set the milestones

Tie each milestone to a clear deliverable or date, not a vague feeling of progress, so there’s no argument about whether it’s been met. A common split is a deposit up front, one or two payments at key stages, and a final payment on completion.

Why it protects you

Milestone billing keeps cash flowing through the project and limits your exposure: if something goes wrong halfway, you’ve already been paid for the work done. It also keeps the client engaged, since each payment marks real, agreed progress.

What goes on each invoice

Each milestone invoice should name the milestone, the amount, and what it represents (e.g. “Milestone 2 of 4: design sign-off — 25% of project fee”), plus the running total billed. Agree the full schedule in writing before you start so every invoice is expected.

Frequently asked questions

What is milestone billing?

Invoicing a project in stages: you split it into defined milestones and bill a portion of the fee as each one is reached, instead of sending a single invoice at the end.

How should I split a project into milestones?

Tie each milestone to a clear deliverable or date. A common structure is a deposit up front, payments at key stages, and a final payment on completion, all agreed in writing first.

Why use milestone billing?

It keeps cash flowing during long projects and limits your risk &mdash; you're paid for completed stages as you go, rather than carrying the whole cost until the end.
