What Is an EIN and Do You Need One?

Tax · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

An EIN (Employer Identification Number) is a US federal tax ID for a business, issued by the IRS. Think of it as a social security number for your company. Here is what it is and whether you need one.

What an EIN is

An EIN is a nine-digit number the IRS uses to identify a business for tax purposes. It’s free to obtain, typically through the IRS, and once issued it stays with the business. It’s the business equivalent of an individual’s SSN.

Who needs one

You generally need an EIN if you have employees, operate as a corporation or partnership, or meet certain other IRS conditions. Many sole proprietors don’t strictly need one, but get an EIN anyway to avoid putting their SSN on forms and invoices.

EIN vs SSN on invoices

A sole proprietor without an EIN would use their SSN for tax forms — which many prefer not to share. An EIN lets you provide a tax ID to clients (for example on a W-9) without exposing your personal SSN, which is the main reason freelancers get one even when not required.

Does it go on your invoice?

An EIN isn’t required on a standard US invoice, since the US has no VAT-style tax number to display. You’ll more often provide it on a W-9 when a business client needs your tax details for reporting. Keep it handy, but your invoice itself usually doesn’t need it.
