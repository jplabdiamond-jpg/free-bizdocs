How Long Should You Keep Invoices and Receipts?

Invoicing · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Invoices aren’t done once they’re paid — tax authorities expect you to keep them for years. Bin them too early and you can’t back up your tax return. Here is the general rule and how to store them.

The general rule

Most countries require you to keep business records, including invoices and receipts, for several years — often in the range of five to seven. The exact period and start point (from the invoice date, the tax year, or filing) are set by your tax authority, so treat the specific number as something to confirm locally.

Why it matters

If you’re ever audited or queried, these records are your evidence — for income declared, VAT reclaimed, and expenses deducted. Without the invoice behind a figure, a deduction or reclaim can be disallowed. Keeping records isn’t bureaucracy; it’s what makes your numbers defensible.

Digital is fine (and better)

In most places you can keep records digitally rather than as paper, as long as they’re complete, legible and retrievable. Scanning or saving PDFs beats a drawer of fading thermal receipts — and some regimes now effectively expect digital records. Keep backups.

Store so you can find them

Retention only helps if you can produce the right document when asked. Keep invoices in order by number and date, name files consistently, and separate by tax year. A few minutes of organisation now saves hours if a query ever lands.
