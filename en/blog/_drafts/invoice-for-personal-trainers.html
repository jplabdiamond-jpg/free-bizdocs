How to Invoice as a Personal Trainer (Sessions, Packages &amp; Memberships)

Industries · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Personal trainers bill in a few ways — single sessions, packages, or monthly memberships — and each needs a slightly different invoice. Here is how to keep billing simple and payments on time.

Match the invoice to how you sell

  • Single sessions — invoice per session or weekly; list dates and the rate.
  • Packages (e.g. 10 sessions) — invoice the package up front and note sessions used/remaining.
  • Memberships — a fixed monthly fee billed on the same date each month.

What to include

Your name and contact details, the client, an itemised list (sessions or the package/membership), the period covered, tax if you are registered, the total and the due date. For packages, showing sessions remaining helps the client and reduces disputes.

Recurring payments and cancellations

For memberships, set up a recurring payment (direct debit or saved card) so you are not chasing every month. Put your cancellation and no-show policy in writing — for example, sessions cancelled with under 24 hours’ notice are chargeable — and reference it on the invoice.

Keep clean records

Number every invoice and keep copies. Consistent records make your tax return straightforward and let you see at a glance who has paid, who is on a package, and who is due to renew.

Frequently asked questions

How do personal trainers invoice clients?

By single session, by multi-session package billed up front, or by a fixed monthly membership. List the sessions or package, the period covered, the total and the due date, and show sessions remaining for packages.

How do I handle monthly membership payments?

Set up a recurring payment such as direct debit or a saved card billed on the same date each month. This avoids chasing payment and keeps income predictable.

Can I charge for late cancellations or no-shows?

Yes, if you have a written policy the client agreed to &mdash; for example charging for sessions cancelled with less than 24 hours' notice. Reference the policy on your invoice so it is clear.
