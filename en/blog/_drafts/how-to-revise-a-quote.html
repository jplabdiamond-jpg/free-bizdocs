How to Revise a Quote (Without Losing the Client)

Quoting · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Quotes change — the client adds scope, or your costs move. The danger isn’t the revision, it’s two quotes floating around with different numbers. Here is how to revise cleanly.

Issue a clear new version

Don’t just resend the old file with numbers changed. Create a new, clearly-labelled version — a revision number or a fresh quote number and today’s date — and say plainly that it replaces the previous one. The client should never have to guess which quote is current.

Explain what changed and why

A silent price jump breeds suspicion. In a line or two, say what changed — added scope, more units, updated material costs — so the new figure reads as fair rather than arbitrary. Tie the change to something concrete the client asked for or that genuinely moved.

Keep versions from colliding

Mark the old quote as superseded in your own records, and reference the version in any acceptance. When the client approves, make sure it’s clear which version they accepted, so the job — and the eventual invoice — matches the right numbers.

When your costs rose

If the change is on your side (materials went up, the job is bigger than first thought), be straightforward about it and, where you can, honour an original quote that’s still within its validity window. A clear expiry date on quotes is what lets you reprice fairly when a client comes back late.

Frequently asked questions

How do I revise a quote I already sent?

Issue a new, clearly-labelled version with its own number or revision and today's date, and state that it replaces the previous quote. Don't quietly edit the old one &mdash; that causes version confusion.

Should I explain why a quote changed?

Yes. A short note on what changed &mdash; added scope, more units, higher material costs &mdash; makes the new price read as fair rather than arbitrary and keeps the client's trust.

Can I increase a quote after sending it?

If it's still within its validity period, it's better to honour it. If scope changed or the quote expired, issue a revised version and explain the reason for the new figure.
