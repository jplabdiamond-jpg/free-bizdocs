What Is a W-9 Form? (For Freelancers &amp; Contractors)

Tax · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

If a US client asks you to “send a W-9 before we pay you,” it’s routine. The W-9 collects your tax details so they can report what they paid you. Here is what it is and why it matters.

What a W-9 is

A W-9 is a short IRS form on which a US business collects a contractor’s name, address and taxpayer ID (SSN or EIN). The client keeps it on file — it isn’t sent to the IRS by itself. It simply gives them the details they need to report payments to you.

Why clients ask for it

US businesses must report payments to contractors over a threshold. To do that they need your tax ID, so they request a W-9 before paying. Providing it promptly keeps your invoices moving; refusing can trigger backup withholding, where they hold back a portion for the IRS.

The W-9 and the 1099

The W-9 is the input; the 1099 is the output. Using the details from your W-9, the client issues you (and the IRS) a 1099 after year-end summarising what they paid. Keeping your W-9 details accurate means your 1099s are correct.

Before you fill one in

Use your EIN instead of your SSN where you can, to avoid sharing your personal number. Confirm the request is from a legitimate client, since a W-9 contains sensitive details. The form itself doesn’t go on your invoice — it’s a separate document you provide to the client.

Frequently asked questions

What is a W-9 form?

Why does a client want a W-9 before paying?
