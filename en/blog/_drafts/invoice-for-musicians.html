How to Invoice as a Musician (Gigs, Sessions &amp; Teaching)

Industries · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Musicians get paid for very different things — gigs, sessions, lessons, licensing — and each bills a little differently. A clear invoice (and a deposit for bookings) keeps the money side simple. Here is how.

Match the invoice to the work

  • Live gigs — invoice a performance fee, often with a deposit to hold the date
  • Session work — bill per hour, per song, or a day rate
  • Teaching — invoice per lesson or a block of lessons
  • Licensing / recording — bill the agreed fee and state the usage granted

Take a deposit for bookings

For gigs and events, a deposit secures the date and protects you against last-minute cancellations. Note the deposit as paid on the final invoice and bill the balance on or before the performance day. Put your cancellation terms in writing.

Expenses and travel

If the booking involves travel, equipment hire or extra musicians, list those separately from your fee so the client sees what’s performance and what’s cost. Agree in advance who covers travel for out-of-town gigs.

Keep clean records

Income from many small sources adds up fast, so number every invoice and keep copies. Good records make your tax return simple and give you a clear picture of which work — gigs, sessions or teaching — actually pays.

Frequently asked questions

How do musicians invoice for a gig?

Invoice a performance fee for the booking, ideally with a deposit to secure the date and the balance due on or before the event. List travel or extra costs separately, and keep each invoice numbered.

Should I take a deposit for bookings?

Yes, for gigs and events a deposit holds the date and protects you against cancellations. Note it as paid on the final invoice and set out your cancellation terms in writing.

How do I invoice for teaching music?

Bill per lesson or sell a block of lessons up front. A numbered invoice with the dates or number of lessons, the rate and the total keeps it clear for both sides.
