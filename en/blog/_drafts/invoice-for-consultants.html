How to Invoice as a Consultant

Industries · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Consulting work gets billed in a few different shapes — by the hour, by project, or on a monthly retainer — and your invoice should make the arrangement obvious. Clear, well-structured invoices also signal the professionalism clients are paying for.

Match the invoice to your billing model

  • Hourly — show hours, rate and a short description per block of work.
  • Fixed project — one line for the agreed fee, or milestone lines (e.g. 50% on start, 50% on delivery).
  • Retainer — a recurring monthly line (“Advisory retainer — October”), with any overage billed separately.

Expenses and disbursements

Bill travel, software or subcontractor costs as separate lines, and keep receipts. Agree in advance whether expenses are billed at cost or with a markup so there are no surprises.

Terms that get you paid

Keep payment terms short (Net 14 is common for consultants), state the actual due date, and for new clients consider a deposit or part-payment up front. Add any tax (sales tax, VAT or GST) as a separate line depending on your country and registration.

Frequently asked questions

How do consultants usually bill clients?

Three common models: hourly (hours × rate), fixed project fee (often split into milestones), and monthly retainer. Your invoice should clearly show which one applies for the period.

Should I charge a deposit as a consultant?

For new clients or larger projects, yes — a deposit or milestone payment up front reduces risk and improves cash flow. State it on a quote or the first invoice.

How do I bill expenses on a consulting invoice?

List travel, software and subcontractor costs as separate lines, keep receipts, and agree beforehand whether they’re billed at cost or with a markup.
