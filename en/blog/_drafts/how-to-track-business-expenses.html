How to Track Business Expenses (A Simple System for Freelancers)

Tax · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Tracking expenses is not busywork — every recorded business cost lowers your taxable profit. A simple, consistent system beats a shoebox of receipts and a stressful tax season. Here is one that works.

Separate business from personal

Open a dedicated business account (or at least one card used only for business). When business money runs through its own account, your records are almost done for you, and the line between deductible and personal spend stays clear.

Capture every receipt

  • Photograph receipts as you get them — paper fades and memories do too.
  • Keep digital copies in one place (a folder or app).
  • For each, note the date, amount, supplier and what it was for.

Use a few clear categories

You do not need dozens. Common ones: software & subscriptions, equipment, travel, marketing, office/home-office, professional fees, and materials. Consistent categories make your totals — and your tax return — fall out at year end.

Know what you can claim, and keep records

Generally, costs that are wholly for the business are deductible; mixed-use items (phone, home office) are claimed on the business-use share. Keep records for the period your tax authority requires — often several years — in case of a query.

Frequently asked questions

What expenses can I deduct?

Costs incurred wholly and exclusively for the business are generally deductible. Mixed personal/business items, like a phone or home office, are claimed on the business-use proportion. Rules vary by country, so check yours.

How long should I keep receipts and records?

Typically several years &mdash; the exact period depends on your country's tax rules. Keep digital copies so nothing fades, and store them so you can find a specific receipt quickly if asked.

Do I need accounting software to track expenses?

Not necessarily. A dedicated business account, photos of receipts, and a simple categorised spreadsheet are enough for many freelancers. Software helps as volume grows, but consistency matters more than the tool.
