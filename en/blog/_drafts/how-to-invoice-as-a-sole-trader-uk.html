How to Invoice as a Sole Trader (UK)

Invoicing · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

As a UK sole trader you invoice clients yourself — there is no special format required, but an invoice should carry a few standard details so it is clear, professional and ready for your Self Assessment. Here is what to include and where VAT fits in.

What a sole trader invoice must show

  • Your name and, if you use one, your trading name.
  • The word “Invoice” and a unique, sequential invoice number.
  • The invoice date (and the date the goods/services were supplied).
  • The client’s name and address.
  • A clear description of what you are charging for, with amounts.
  • The total due, your payment terms and how to pay (bank details).

Do you charge VAT?

Only if you are VAT-registered. Registration is compulsory once your taxable turnover passes the VAT threshold (£90,000), and optional below it. If you are registered, show your VAT number and the VAT per rate, and the invoice becomes a VAT invoice. If you are not registered, you simply do not add VAT — and you should not show a VAT number or imply VAT is included.

Numbering and records

Give every invoice a unique number in sequence (0001, 0002…) so none are missing or duplicated. Keep copies of your invoices and records: HMRC expects you to keep business records for your Self Assessment, generally for at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline of the relevant tax year.

Getting paid on time

State clear terms (for example “payment due within 14 days”). UK businesses also have a statutory right to charge interest and a fixed recovery sum on late commercial payments, which you can mention in your terms to encourage prompt payment.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to charge VAT as a sole trader?

Only if you are VAT-registered, which is required once your taxable turnover passes the £90,000 threshold and optional below it. If you are not registered, do not add VAT or show a VAT number.

Do sole trader invoices need an invoice number?

Yes. Each invoice should have a unique, sequential number so your records are complete and nothing is missing or duplicated.

How long should I keep my invoices?

HMRC generally expects sole traders to keep business records for at least 5 years after the 31 January Self Assessment deadline for that tax year.
