How to Handle a Partial Payment on an Invoice

Getting paid · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

A client pays part of an invoice — now what? The key is to record it clearly so the remaining balance never gets lost. Here is how to handle a partial payment cleanly.

Record what was paid

Mark the invoice as part-paid, not paid: note the amount received, the date, and the balance still due. The invoice stays open for the remainder. Clear records here prevent the classic mistake of treating a part-payment as settlement and forgetting the rest.

Show the balance clearly

Send the client an updated position: original total, amount paid, balance outstanding, and the date that balance is due. Whether on a reissued invoice marked part-paid or a short statement, make the remaining number unmistakable so there’s no “I thought we were square”.

Allocating across invoices

If a client owes several invoices and pays a lump that doesn’t match any of them, decide how to allocate it — ideally against the oldest invoice first — and tell them which invoice you’ve applied it to. Unallocated payments are how balances drift out of sync.

Chase the rest

Treat the outstanding balance like any overdue amount: a polite reminder near the due date, then firmer follow-up if needed. If partial payments are becoming a pattern with a client, consider deposits or a payment plan so expectations are set before the next job.

Frequently asked questions

How do I record a partial payment?

Mark the invoice part-paid rather than paid: note the amount received, the date, and the balance still due. The invoice stays open for the remainder so the balance isn't lost.

How do I show the remaining balance to the client?

Send an updated position showing the original total, amount paid, balance outstanding and the due date for it &mdash; on a reissued invoice marked part-paid or a short statement, with the remaining amount clear.

A client paid a lump sum across several invoices &mdash; how do I apply it?

Allocate it, ideally against the oldest invoice first, and tell the client which invoice you applied it to. Unallocated payments cause balances to drift out of sync.
