What Are Recurring Invoices? (And When to Use Them)

Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

A recurring invoice bills a customer the same (or similar) amount on a regular schedule — weekly, monthly or yearly — without you creating each one from scratch. They’re ideal for subscriptions, retainers and any ongoing service.

When recurring invoices make sense

  • Monthly retainers (consulting, design, marketing, VA work).
  • Subscription or membership fees.
  • Regular services — cleaning, maintenance, tutoring, hosting.

How they work

You set the client, the amount, the schedule and a start date; the invoice is then issued automatically each cycle with a new number and date. Some tools also charge a saved payment method automatically, which turns the invoice into a subscription.

Keep them accurate

Review recurring invoices periodically — usage, scope or prices change. Make it easy for clients to see what each cycle covers, and send any price changes in advance.

Frequently asked questions

What is a recurring invoice?

An invoice that bills the same client a set amount on a regular schedule — weekly, monthly or yearly — issued automatically each cycle so you don’t recreate it each time.

When should I use recurring invoices?

For retainers, subscriptions, memberships and regular services like cleaning, maintenance or tutoring — anything billed on a predictable schedule.

What is the difference between a recurring invoice and a subscription?

A recurring invoice requests payment each cycle; a subscription also charges a saved payment method automatically. A subscription is essentially a recurring invoice with auto-payment.
