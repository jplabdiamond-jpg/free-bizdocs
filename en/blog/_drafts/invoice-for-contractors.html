How to Invoice as a Contractor (Trades & Construction)

Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Contractor invoicing has a few wrinkles other trades don’t: progress payments across a long job, materials billed separately from labour, and sometimes retention held back until completion. Get these right and you’ll keep cash flowing on bigger jobs.

Separate labour, materials and other costs

List labour (hours or a fixed amount), materials, equipment hire and any disposal or permit fees as separate lines. Clients and their accountants find the breakdown easier to approve, and it makes variations simpler to add.

Progress payments and milestones

For long jobs, invoice in stages tied to milestones (for example deposit, first fix, second fix, completion) rather than waiting until the end. Reference the stage and the contract on each invoice so it’s clear what’s being billed.

Retention, variations and tax

If the contract holds retention, show it as a deduction and note when it’s released. Add approved variations as their own lines. Apply the right tax for your country — US sales tax rules differ for materials vs labour by state; UK/AU/CA add VAT/GST once registered (watch for construction-specific VAT rules in the UK).

Frequently asked questions

How do contractors invoice for progress payments?

Invoice in stages tied to agreed milestones (deposit, first fix, completion, etc.) rather than only at the end. Reference the stage and the contract on each invoice so the client knows exactly what they’re paying for.

Should materials and labour be on separate lines?

Yes. Listing labour, materials, hire and fees separately makes the invoice easier to approve and keeps variations clear. It also helps with tax, which can treat materials and labour differently.

What is retention on a contractor invoice?

Retention is a percentage the client holds back until the job is complete (or a defects period ends). Show it as a deduction on the invoice and note when it will be released.
