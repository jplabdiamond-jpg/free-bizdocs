Quote vs Invoice: What&rsquo;s the Difference?

Quoting · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

A quote and an invoice look similar but do opposite jobs: one is an offer before the work, the other a bill after it. Mixing them up confuses clients. Here is the difference, simply.

The core difference

A quote is issued before the work: it proposes a price and terms, and the client decides whether to accept. An invoice is issued after (or at agreed points during) the work: it states what is now owed and requests payment. One asks “shall we?”; the other says “please pay”.

When to send each

  • Send a quote when a client asks what something will cost, before committing.
  • Send an invoice once the work (or a billed stage) is complete and payment is due.

How a quote becomes an invoice

Because both describe the same job, an accepted quote converts neatly into an invoice — same line items, now billed. Keep the numbers consistent so the client sees they’re being charged exactly what was agreed. A one-click convert avoids re-keying and mistakes.

Don't confuse the two

A quote is not a demand for payment, and an invoice is not a negotiation. Label each clearly and give each its own number sequence, so there’s never doubt about whether a document is a proposal or a bill.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a quote and an invoice?

A quote proposes a price before the work and the client accepts or declines it. An invoice is sent after the work to request payment for what is owed. One is an offer, the other a bill.

Does a quote become an invoice?

Effectively, yes. Once a quote is accepted and the work is done, the same line items are billed as an invoice. Keeping the figures identical shows the client they're charged what was agreed.

Can I ask for payment on a quote?

A quote itself isn't a payment request. If you want money up front, agree a deposit and issue a deposit invoice; the quote stays a proposal of the overall price.
