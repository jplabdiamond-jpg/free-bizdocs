How to Register for VAT: A Simple Overview

Tax · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Registering for VAT is a milestone for a growing business. It becomes mandatory above a threshold, and optional below it. Here is a plain overview of when and how — check your own country’s tax authority for the specifics.

When you must register

Most countries set a turnover threshold: once your taxable sales over a rolling period cross it, registration is compulsory. Watch your running total as you grow — registering late can mean owing VAT you never charged. The threshold amount and period are set by your tax authority.

Registering voluntarily

Below the threshold you can often register voluntarily. It’s worth it if you buy a lot of VATable supplies (you can reclaim that VAT) or if looking VAT-registered matters to your business clients. The trade-off is adding VAT to your prices and filing returns.

What you need

Registration is usually an online application with your business details, activity, and expected turnover. Once approved you receive a VAT number. From that point you charge VAT on applicable sales, can reclaim VAT on purchases, and file periodic returns.

What changes on your invoices

After registering, your invoices become VAT invoices: they must show your VAT number, the VAT rate, the VAT amount, and totals before and after VAT. Build these into your invoice template so every bill you send is compliant from day one.

Frequently asked questions

When do I have to register for VAT?

When your taxable turnover crosses your country's VAT threshold over the relevant period. The exact amount and rules are set by your tax authority, so monitor your running total and check their guidance.

Can I register for VAT voluntarily?

Usually yes, below the threshold. It can pay off if you buy a lot of VATable supplies you could reclaim, or if being VAT-registered matters to your business clients, at the cost of charging VAT and filing returns.

What changes after I register for VAT?

You charge VAT on applicable sales, can reclaim VAT on purchases, and file returns. Your invoices must show your VAT number, the rate, the VAT amount and the net and gross totals.
