Purchase Order vs Contract: What&rsquo;s the Difference?

Purchasing · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Is a purchase order a contract? Sort of — and the relationship between the two trips people up. Here is how POs and contracts relate, and which one governs a deal.

What each one is

A purchase order is a buyer’s formal offer to buy specific goods or services at a stated price. A contract is a broader agreement setting out the full terms of a relationship — obligations, liability, warranties, duration. A PO is transactional; a contract is the framework.

Is a PO legally binding?

On its own, a PO is an offer. Once the supplier accepts it (by confirming or by fulfilling the order), it generally forms a binding agreement for that purchase. So a PO can create a contract — but it’s usually a narrow one, covering just that order, not the wider terms a full contract would.

When each is used

  • A PO suits one-off or repeat purchases of defined goods at a known price
  • A contract suits ongoing relationships, services, or anything needing detailed terms (liability, IP, confidentiality)

How they work together

Often both exist: a master contract sets the terms, and individual POs order against it. If they ever conflict, the contract usually says which prevails — so for anything beyond a simple purchase, the contract is where the real protection lives, with the PO handling the ordering.
