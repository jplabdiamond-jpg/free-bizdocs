How to Invoice as an Independent Contractor (US)

Invoicing · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

As a US independent contractor you send clients an invoice to get paid. There is no official form — you just need a clear, itemised document with the right details so clients can pay you quickly and you can report the income at tax time.

What to include

  • Your name or business name and contact details.
  • Invoice number and invoice date.
  • The client’s name and address.
  • A description of the work, hours or units, your rate and the total.
  • Payment terms (for example Net 15/Net 30) and how to pay.

Do not print your Social Security Number on the invoice. If a client needs your taxpayer ID, give it on a Form W-9 sent securely, not on every invoice.

Do contractors charge sales tax?

Usually not on services — most states do not tax professional services — but it varies by state, and selling physical goods can be taxable. If you are unsure whether your service is taxable where you operate, check your state’s rules or a tax professional.

W-9 vs invoice vs 1099

These are three different things: the W-9 is a one-time form giving the client your taxpayer details; the invoice is your request for payment for each job; the 1099-NEC is the form the client files (and sends you) after year end if they paid you $600 or more. You still report all your income whether or not you receive a 1099.

Getting paid faster

Send the invoice promptly, keep terms short and clear, and number your invoices so nothing slips. A clean PDF with your payment details reduces back-and-forth.

Frequently asked questions

Do independent contractors charge sales tax on invoices?

Usually not on professional services, but it varies by state and taxable goods can be an exception. Check your state’s rules if you are unsure.

Should I put my SSN on an invoice?

No. Avoid putting your Social Security Number on invoices. If a client needs your taxpayer ID, provide it once on a Form W-9 sent securely.

What is the difference between a 1099 and an invoice?

The invoice is your request for payment for a job. The 1099-NEC is the tax form a client files after year end if they paid you $600 or more.
