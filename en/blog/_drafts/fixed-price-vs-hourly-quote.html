Fixed Price vs Hourly: Which Should You Quote?

Quoting · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

One of the first choices on any quote is fixed price or hourly. Each suits different jobs, and each has a trap. Here is how to pick the right one and protect yourself.

Fixed price

You quote one agreed figure for the whole job. Clients love the certainty, and if you work efficiently your effective rate goes up. The risk is scope creep: if the job grows, you absorb it unless you’ve defined exactly what’s included. Best for well-defined work you can scope confidently.

Hourly

You bill for time actually worked. It’s fair when the scope is unclear or likely to change, and you’re never underpaid for extra work. The downside is the client has less certainty on the total, so they may want a cap or regular updates. Best for open-ended, exploratory, or shifting work.

How to choose

  • Well-defined deliverable? Lean fixed price.
  • Unknowns or likely changes? Lean hourly, or hourly with an estimate.
  • Client wants certainty? Fixed price with a tight scope.

Protect yourself either way

For fixed price, write down exactly what’s included and what counts as extra, so changes trigger a new quote. For hourly, offer an estimate and a cap or checkpoint so the client isn’t anxious about an open meter. Either way, the protection is a clear written scope.

Frequently asked questions

Should I quote fixed price or hourly?

Fixed price suits well-defined jobs you can scope confidently and gives the client certainty. Hourly suits unclear or changing work and ensures you're paid for all your time. Match the model to how defined the job is.

How do I avoid scope creep on a fixed-price quote?

Write down exactly what the price includes and what counts as extra. When the client asks for something outside that scope, treat it as a new quote rather than absorbing it.

Will clients accept an hourly quote?

Many will, especially for unclear work, if you give an estimate of the total and offer a cap or regular check-ins so they aren't worried about an open-ended bill.
