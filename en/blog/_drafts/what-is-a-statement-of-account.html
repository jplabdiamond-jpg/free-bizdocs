What Is a Statement of Account? (And When to Send One)

Getting paid · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

A statement of account is a summary of everything a client owes — invoices issued, payments received, and the outstanding balance. It’s a gentle, effective way to prompt payment. Here is how to use one.

What it is

Unlike an invoice, which bills for one job, a statement lists all activity on a client’s account over a period: each invoice, each payment, and the running balance. It doesn’t replace invoices — it summarises them, so the client sees the whole picture at a glance.

Statement vs invoice

An invoice is a demand for payment of a specific amount for specific work. A statement is a recap: it doesn’t introduce new charges, it restates what’s already been invoiced and shows what’s still outstanding. Clients pay from invoices; statements remind them what’s open.

When to send one

  • Monthly, to clients you bill regularly
  • When several invoices are outstanding and you want one clear total
  • As a polite nudge before a firmer reminder

How it helps you get paid

A statement is non-confrontational — it’s just “here’s where your account stands” — but it quietly surfaces anything forgotten. Clients often pay an overlooked invoice the moment a statement shows it still open. Include an ageing breakdown (current, 30, 60, 90 days) to make overdue amounts obvious.

Frequently asked questions

What is a statement of account?

A summary of a client's invoices, payments and outstanding balance over a period. It doesn't bill new charges &mdash; it recaps what's already been invoiced and shows what's still owed.

What's the difference between a statement and an invoice?

An invoice demands payment for specific work; a statement summarises all invoices and payments on a client's account and shows the outstanding balance. Clients pay from invoices, not statements.

When should I send a statement of account?

Commonly monthly to regular clients, when several invoices are outstanding, or as a gentle nudge before a firmer reminder. An ageing breakdown makes overdue amounts stand out.
