How to Invoice as a Virtual Assistant

Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

As a virtual assistant you often juggle several clients and billing models at once, so a clean, consistent invoice per client — sent on a regular schedule — is what keeps the admin light and the payments flowing.

Hourly, package or retainer

  • Hourly — log time per task and show hours × rate.
  • Package — a set number of hours per month at a fixed price.
  • Retainer — a recurring monthly fee for ongoing availability, with overage billed separately.

Track time and show the work

Keep a simple log of hours or tasks per client so each invoice reflects what you actually did. Grouping work into a few clear lines (e.g. “Inbox & calendar management”, “Research”) reads better than a long list of tiny entries.

Terms and tax

Invoice on the same day each month, keep terms short (Net 7–14), and bill in your client’s currency where it makes sense. Apply sales tax, VAT or GST only if you’re registered in your country.

Frequently asked questions

How do virtual assistants usually bill?

By the hour, by a monthly package of hours, or on a retainer for ongoing availability. Pick one model per client and send a consistent invoice each cycle.

How do I track time for VA invoices?

Keep a simple per-client log of hours or tasks, then group the work into a few clear lines on the invoice rather than many tiny entries.

Should a VA invoice monthly?

Monthly billing on a set day keeps cash flow predictable and admin low. Use short terms (Net 7–14) and make payment easy.
