How to Write a Payment Reminder Email (Templates)

Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Chasing payment feels awkward, but most late invoices aren’t refusals — they’re forgotten. A short, friendly, well-timed email usually fixes it. Here are templates for each stage, from a gentle nudge to a firm final notice.

Before you send anything

Make sure the invoice was clear in the first place: a due date (not just “Net 30”), the amount, and easy payment options. Then follow a simple cadence — a reminder a few days before the due date, one on the day, and follow-ups at about one and two weeks overdue.

1. Friendly reminder (a few days before due)

Subject: Invoice INV-0042 — due 20 Nov
Hi [Name], just a quick note that invoice INV-0042 for $[amount] is due on [date]. Payment details are on the invoice (attached). Thanks so much — happy to answer any questions.

2. On the due date

Subject: Invoice INV-0042 is due today
Hi [Name], a friendly reminder that invoice INV-0042 for $[amount] is due today. If it’s already on its way, thank you! If not, you can pay via [method]. Let me know if anything’s unclear.

3. Overdue (1–2 weeks past due)

Subject: Invoice INV-0042 — now overdue
Hi [Name], invoice INV-0042 for $[amount] was due on [date] and is now [X] days overdue. Could you let me know when I can expect payment? If there’s a problem with the invoice, I’m glad to sort it out. Payment details are attached.

4. Final notice

Keep it professional and specific: restate the amount and original due date, note any late fee stated in your terms, give a firm new date, and say what happens next. Avoid anger — clarity gets paid; emotion rarely does.

Frequently asked questions

How do I politely ask for payment?

Keep it short, friendly and specific: reference the invoice number and amount, state the due date, and make paying easy. Assume good faith — most late payments are simply forgotten.

When should I send a payment reminder?

A good cadence is a reminder a few days before the due date, one on the day, then follow-ups around one and two weeks overdue, escalating in firmness each time.

Can I charge a late fee?

Yes, if you stated it in your terms before the work (for example 1.5% per month). Reference that term in your reminder rather than adding a surprise fee.
