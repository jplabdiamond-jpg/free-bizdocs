What to Do When a Client Won&rsquo;t Pay an Invoice

Getting paid · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

An unpaid invoice is stressful, but most get resolved with a calm, escalating series of reminders. Panic and anger rarely get you paid faster. Here is a sequence that stays professional and usually works.

1. The friendly reminder

A day or two after the due date, send a short, polite nudge. Assume it was simply overlooked — often it was. Re-attach the invoice, restate the amount and due date, and make it easy to pay. Keep the tone warm; you are reminding, not accusing.

2. The firm follow-up

About a week later, send a clearer message. State that the invoice is now overdue, give a specific new deadline, and ask them to confirm payment. Reference your payment terms. If there is a problem on their end, this is usually when it surfaces — so invite them to tell you.

3. The final notice

If it is still unpaid, send a formal final notice. State the amount, how overdue it is, any late fee that now applies if your terms allow one, and a firm final date for payment. Keep it factual and unemotional — a final notice carries more weight when it is calm.

4. What comes next

Beyond that, options include pausing further work, a formal demand letter, mediation, a small-claims process, or a collections service, depending on the amount and your location. These are last resorts — and the best protection is prevention: clear terms up front, a deposit on larger jobs, and invoicing promptly.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I wait before chasing an invoice?

Send a gentle reminder a day or two after the due date, a firmer one about a week later, and a formal final notice after that. Prompt, polite follow-up resolves most late payments.

Can I charge a late fee?

Often yes, if you stated it in your terms before the work. A reasonable late fee or interest, set out on the invoice and in your terms, is a common and fair way to encourage payment.

Should I keep working for a client who hasn't paid?

Usually it is reasonable to pause new work until overdue invoices are settled. Make that clear politely, and get a deposit before resuming larger projects.
