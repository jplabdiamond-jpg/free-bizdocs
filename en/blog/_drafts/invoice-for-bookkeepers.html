How to Invoice as a Bookkeeper

Industries · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

As a bookkeeper, your own invoices should set the standard. Most of the work is recurring, which makes billing clean — if you set it up right. Here is how to invoice your bookkeeping clients.

Retainer vs hourly

Most bookkeeping is billed as a monthly fixed fee for an agreed scope (so many transactions, so many accounts), which gives both sides predictability. Hourly suits ad-hoc work or clients whose volume varies. Decide which per client, and state what the fee covers so scope doesn’t quietly expand.

One-off clean-up projects

A new client with messy or backlogged books is a project, not a monthly fee. Bill the catch-up separately — a fixed project price or hourly — before the recurring arrangement starts. Taking a deposit on a big clean-up is reasonable given the effort involved.

List services clearly

Clients value clarity — it’s your profession. On a monthly invoice, state the period and what’s included (“Bookkeeping — March 2026”). Put extra work (year-end prep, a one-off report) on its own line so the client sees the base fee and the extras distinctly.

Automate the recurring billing

Because the same invoice goes out every month, use recurring invoices so they issue on schedule without re-keying. Consistent monthly invoicing, like you’d advise your own clients, keeps your cash flow steady and your records tidy. Number every invoice and keep copies.
