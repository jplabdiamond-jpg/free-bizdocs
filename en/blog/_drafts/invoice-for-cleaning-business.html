How to Invoice for a Cleaning Business

Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Cleaning work is often recurring — weekly offices, fortnightly homes, end-of-tenancy one-offs — so the real win is invoicing on a predictable schedule that gets you paid without chasing. Here’s how to set it up.

Per-visit, hourly or monthly

Decide how you bill: a fixed price per visit, an hourly rate, or a monthly amount for a set schedule. For recurring clients, a single monthly invoice listing each visit (or a flat monthly fee) is usually cleanest.

What to itemise

Show the service and dates (e.g. “Office clean — 4 visits in October”), any extras like carpet or window cleaning as separate lines, and supplies if you bill them. Note the property or site so multi-site clients can match invoices.

Terms and tax

Keep terms short and consistent (Net 7 or Net 14 works well for recurring work), send the invoice on the same day each cycle, and make payment easy. Apply sales tax, VAT or GST depending on your country and registration.

Frequently asked questions

How do I invoice recurring cleaning clients?

Invoice on a fixed cycle — for example one monthly invoice listing each visit, or a flat monthly fee. Sending it the same day each cycle with short terms (Net 7–14) keeps payments predictable.

Should I charge per visit or per hour for cleaning?

Either works. A fixed price per visit is simple for clients to budget; hourly suits variable jobs. Pick one per client and show it clearly on the invoice.

Do I charge tax on cleaning services?

It depends on your country and registration. Some US states tax cleaning services; in the UK, Australia and Canada you add VAT/GST once registered. Show it as a separate line.
