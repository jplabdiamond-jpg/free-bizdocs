How to Invoice as a Graphic Designer

Industries · Free BizDoc · __PUBDATE_EN__

Design invoices are about more than the fee — they’re where you set expectations on revisions, usage rights and when files are handed over. A tidy invoice with the right notes prevents the most common payment disputes in creative work.

Deposit first, files on final payment

Take a deposit before starting (often 30–50%), and release final, print-ready or source files only after the balance is paid. State this on your quote and invoice so it’s understood up front.

Revisions and scope

Note how many revision rounds are included and your rate for extra rounds, then bill additional revisions as their own line. This keeps “just one more tweak” from eroding your time for free.

Usage, licensing and tax

If you’re licensing artwork rather than selling it outright, say so in the notes (medium, territory, duration). Apply the right tax for your country — in the US, design services are taxable in some states; in the UK/AU/CA you add VAT/GST once registered. Design fees can also be subject to withholding in some countries when paid to individuals.

Frequently asked questions

Should graphic designers take a deposit?

Yes — a deposit of around 30–50% before starting is standard. It commits the client and protects your time, with final files released after the balance is paid.

How do I charge for extra design revisions?

State the number of revision rounds included, then bill extra rounds as a separate line at an agreed rate. Putting this on the quote and invoice prevents scope creep.

Do I charge tax on design work?

It depends on your country and registration. In the US, taxability of design services varies by state; in the UK, Australia and Canada you add VAT/GST once registered. Show tax as a separate line.
