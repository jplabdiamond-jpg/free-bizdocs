Estimate vs Firm Quote: What&rsquo;s the Difference?

Quoting · Free BizDoc · Nov 22, 2026

An estimate and a firm quote sound similar but commit you to very different things: one is a best guess, the other a fixed price you’re bound to. Confusing them causes disputes. Here is the difference.

The key difference

An estimate is an approximate figure — your best assessment of likely cost, which may move as the job becomes clearer. A firm quote is a fixed commitment: accept it and that’s the price, barring agreed changes. The difference is whether the number can move.

When to use each

  • Use an estimate when the scope is unclear or the work is exploratory
  • Use a firm quote when you can scope the job confidently and the client wants certainty

Giving a firm quote on genuinely unpredictable work is how you end up absorbing overruns.

Label it clearly

The danger is a client treating your estimate as a quote and holding you to the penny. So say which it is, in writing, on the document — “Estimate (approximate, subject to change)” vs “Quotation (fixed for 30 days)”. A word of clarity now prevents an argument later.

If an estimate changes

When an estimate needs to move, tell the client as soon as you know, not at invoicing. Explain what changed and give an updated figure. Clients accept a well-communicated change to an estimate; what they won’t accept is a surprise on the final bill.
