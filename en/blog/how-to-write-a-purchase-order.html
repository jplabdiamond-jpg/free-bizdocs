How to Write a Purchase Order (With a Free Template)

Purchasing · Free BizDoc · Nov 8, 2026

A purchase order (PO) is the document a buyer sends to a supplier to formally order goods or services at an agreed price. It is the buyer’s side of the deal, issued before the invoice. Here is how to write a clear one.

What a purchase order is for

A PO records exactly what you are ordering, at what price, before anything is delivered or billed. It protects both sides: the supplier knows precisely what to supply, and you have a written record to check the eventual invoice against. The supplier confirms it, and the order is agreed.

What to include

  • The words Purchase Order and a unique PO number
  • Your business details (the buyer) and the supplier’s details
  • Order date and any required delivery date
  • An itemised list: description, quantity, unit price and line total
  • Subtotal, tax if applicable, and the order total
  • Delivery address and any agreed terms

The PO number is the key

The PO number ties everything together. When the supplier later invoices you, they quote your PO number, and you can match the invoice to the approved order in seconds. Many companies won’t pay an invoice that has no matching PO, so a clear number matters.

Why bother issuing one?

POs give you control: budgets are approved before money is committed, deliveries can be checked against the order, and invoices can be matched before payment. Even for a one-person business, issuing a PO for larger orders prevents surprises and disputes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a purchase order and an invoice?

The buyer issues the purchase order to order goods at an agreed price. The supplier later issues the invoice to request payment, usually quoting the PO number. The PO comes first.

What must a purchase order include?

A PO number, the buyer and supplier details, the order date, an itemised list with quantities and prices, the total, the delivery address, and any agreed terms.

Do small businesses need purchase orders?

They are optional, but issuing POs for larger or recurring orders gives you a clear record to match invoices against and keeps spending under control.
