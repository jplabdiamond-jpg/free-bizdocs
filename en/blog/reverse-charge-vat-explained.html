Reverse Charge VAT, Explained Simply

Tax · Free BizDoc · Nov 9, 2026

Under the reverse charge, the buyer — not the seller — accounts for the VAT on a sale. It sounds complicated, but the idea is simple. Here is what it means and what to put on the invoice.

The basic idea

Normally the seller charges VAT and pays it to the tax authority. Under the reverse charge, the seller invoices without adding VAT, and the buyer records both the output and input VAT on their own return. For many fully-taxable buyers the two cancel out, which is the point — it reduces fraud and simplifies cross-border trade.

When it typically applies

Reverse charge commonly comes up on certain cross-border B2B services, and in some countries on specific domestic sectors (for example construction) and goods prone to fraud. The exact scope depends entirely on your country’s rules, so this is where to confirm the detail locally.

What to put on the invoice

On a reverse-charge invoice you show no VAT amount, but you must include a note making it clear the reverse charge applies — for example “Reverse charge: customer to account for VAT” — and usually both your VAT number and the customer’s. The exact wording is set by local rules.

Why it exists

Moving the VAT accounting to the buyer removes the chance for a seller to collect VAT and disappear, and it spares foreign suppliers from having to register in every country they sell services into. For you, the practical effect is an invoice with no VAT line but a clear reverse-charge note.

Frequently asked questions

Who pays VAT under the reverse charge?

The buyer accounts for it on their own VAT return, instead of the seller charging it. The seller's invoice shows no VAT but states that the reverse charge applies.

What do I write on a reverse-charge invoice?

Show no VAT amount, add a note such as 'Reverse charge: customer to account for VAT', and usually include both parties' VAT numbers. Follow your country's exact required wording.

When does reverse charge apply?

Commonly on some cross-border B2B services and, in certain countries, specific domestic sectors like construction. The scope depends on local rules, so confirm with your tax authority.
