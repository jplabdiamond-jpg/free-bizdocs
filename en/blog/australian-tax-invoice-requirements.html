Australian Tax Invoice Requirements (ABN & GST)

Free BizDoc · Oct 21, 2026

In Australia, if you’re registered for GST you issue a tax invoice so your customers can claim GST credits. The ATO sets out specific details a tax invoice must show — and a couple of them differ depending on the sale amount.

What a tax invoice must include

  • The words “Tax Invoice”.
  • Your business or trading name and your ABN.
  • The date the invoice was issued.
  • A description of the items sold, including quantity and price.
  • The GST amount (or a statement that the total includes GST).
  • The extent to which each sale is taxable.

Sales of $1,000 or more

For taxable sales of $1,000 or more (including GST), the tax invoice must also show the buyer’s identity or ABN. For sales under $1,000 that detail isn’t required, though it’s fine to include it.

How GST works on the invoice

GST is 10% on most goods and services. You can show it as a separate line, or state that the total price includes GST — for a single-rate invoice, “Total price includes GST” is acceptable. Some items are GST-free; show those clearly so the GST total is correct.

This is general guidance, not tax advice. GST rules can change — check current requirements on the ATO website or with your accountant.

Frequently asked questions

What must an Australian tax invoice show?

The words “Tax Invoice”, your name and ABN, the date, a description with quantity and price, the GST amount (or that the total includes GST), and how much of the sale is taxable. For sales of $1,000 or more it must also show the buyer’s identity or ABN.

Do I need an ABN to issue a tax invoice?

Yes. A valid tax invoice must show your ABN. Without one, the buyer generally can’t claim GST credits and may have to withhold tax from the payment.

When do I need the buyer’s details on a tax invoice?

For taxable sales of $1,000 or more (including GST), the tax invoice must include the buyer’s identity or ABN. For smaller sales it’s optional.
