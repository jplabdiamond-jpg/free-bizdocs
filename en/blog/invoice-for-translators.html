How to Invoice as a Translator (Per Word, Per Hour &amp; Minimums)

Industries · Free BizDoc · Nov 25, 2026

Translators bill in a few ways — per word, per hour, or per project — and often for clients abroad. A clear invoice and the right setup for cross-border payment keep it simple. Here is how.

Choose your billing basis

  • Per word — the common standard; state the rate and word count (source or target)
  • Per hour — for editing, proofreading or revision work
  • Per project — a fixed price for a defined document

Whatever you use, show the basis and the maths so the total is transparent.

Minimums and rush rates

Small jobs aren’t worth it at a pure per-word rate, so set a minimum fee and state it up front. For tight deadlines, a rush rate (a percentage uplift) is standard — put it on its own line so the client sees what the premium is for.

Agencies vs direct clients

Agencies often have their own PO numbers and longer payment terms — quote their PO on the invoice and expect Net 30+. Direct clients are usually faster and may need a payment link. Confirm each client’s process up front so invoices don’t stall in their system.

Cross-border payment

Much translation work is international, so decide the invoice currency, note the exchange rate for your records, and offer a payment method that works across borders. Agree who covers transfer fees — for small jobs, build them into your rate so they don’t erode the fee.
