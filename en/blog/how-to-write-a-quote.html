How to Write a Quote (With a Free Template)

Quoting · Free BizDoc · Oct 28, 2026

A quote (or quotation) gives a client a firm price for a job before any work starts. Unlike a rough estimate, a quote is a price you commit to, so it pays to be clear about exactly what is included.

What to include in a quote

  • Your business details and the client’s details.
  • A quote number and date, and a valid-until date.
  • An itemised scope of work with prices.
  • The total, and any tax if you are registered.
  • An acceptance line so the client can confirm.

Quote vs estimate

An estimate is an approximate figure that can change; a quote is a fixed price the client can accept as-is. Use an estimate when the scope is uncertain and a quote when you can commit to the number.

Set a valid-until date

Prices and availability change, so put a valid-until date (commonly 30 days) on every quote. It protects you if costs move and nudges the client to decide.
