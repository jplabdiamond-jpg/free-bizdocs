How Long Is a Quote Valid? (And How to Set an Expiry)

Quoting · Free BizDoc · Nov 9, 2026

A quote is valid for as long as you say it is — there is no fixed legal period. Setting a clear validity window protects you from rising costs and nudges the client to decide. Here is how to do it well.

You set the validity period

Put a line like “This quote is valid for 30 days” or an explicit expiry date on every quote. Until the client accepts it, a quote is an offer, and you are entitled to say how long that offer stands.

Typical periods

  • 14–30 days is common for most services and small jobs.
  • 7 days suits work where your costs (materials, availability) move quickly.
  • 60–90 days may fit large projects with longer decision cycles — but only if your costs are stable.

Why an expiry protects you

Prices change — materials, your availability, your rates. Without an expiry, a client could accept a six-month-old quote at the old price and expect you to honour it. A validity window lets you re-price fairly if they come back later.

When a quote expires

If a client accepts after the expiry, you are free to requote. Usually the simplest move is to issue a fresh quote with today’s date and current pricing. If nothing has changed, you can simply confirm the same figures still stand.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a legal time limit on a quote?

No. You decide how long a quote is valid and state it on the document. Until the client accepts, it is an offer you can set an expiry on or withdraw.

What happens if a client accepts an expired quote?

You are not obliged to honour the old price. You can issue a fresh quote with current pricing, or confirm the same figures if nothing has changed.

How long should I make a quote valid?

14 to 30 days works for most jobs. Use a shorter window if your material or labour costs move quickly, and a longer one only if your costs are stable.
