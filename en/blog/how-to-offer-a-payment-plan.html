How to Offer a Payment Plan to a Client (Without Getting Burned)

Getting paid · Free BizDoc · Nov 7, 2026

A payment plan can turn a stalled invoice into cash and keep a good client. The key is to structure it clearly and put it in writing so both sides know exactly what is due and when.

When a payment plan makes sense

Offer instalments when the amount is large, the client is otherwise reliable, or cash flow on their side is temporary. For brand-new clients, prefer a deposit up front and keep the remaining balance short. A plan is a tool to get paid — not a reason to keep working unpaid indefinitely.

Structure it clearly

  • Deposit to start (e.g. 30–50%), then equal instalments on fixed dates.
  • Tie instalments to milestones for project work, so payment and delivery stay in step.
  • Keep the schedule short — a few payments, not many.
  • Decide what happens if an instalment is missed (pause work, late fee).

Put it in writing

A one-page agreement is enough: total amount, each instalment and date, the method, and the consequence of a missed payment. Have the client confirm in writing before you continue. This prevents “I thought it was less” later.

Invoice each instalment

Issue a separate invoice for each payment, numbered and dated, referencing the plan (e.g. “Instalment 2 of 3”). That keeps your records clean and makes each due date its own clear reminder.

Frequently asked questions

Should I take a deposit before starting a payment plan?

Usually yes. A deposit of 30&ndash;50% confirms commitment and reduces your risk, especially for new clients. The remaining balance is then split into a short set of instalments.

How do I invoice instalments?

Raise a separate, numbered invoice for each instalment with its own due date, and reference the plan (for example 'Instalment 2 of 3'). This keeps bookkeeping clean and each payment easy to track.

What if the client misses an instalment?

Apply whatever you agreed in writing &mdash; pause further work, send a reminder, or add a disclosed late fee. Agreeing the consequence up front makes it easy to act without conflict.
