How to Invoice as a Handyman (Labour, Materials &amp; Call-Outs)

Industries · Free BizDoc · Nov 11, 2026

A handyman’s invoice works best when it separates labour, materials and any call-out fee and ties back to the job. That transparency builds trust and gets you paid faster. Here is what to include.

What to include

  • Your business name and contact details (and any licence or insurance reference)
  • The client and the job address, which is often not the billing address
  • An itemised split: labour (hourly or fixed), materials, and any call-out fee
  • Tax if you are registered, a clear total, and the due date

Hourly vs fixed price

For small, unpredictable repairs, an hourly rate with materials on top is fair and easy to justify — show the hours and rate. For a defined job (hang a door, build flat-pack, fit a shelf), a fixed price agreed up front is clearer and avoids any argument over hours.

Deposits on bigger jobs

For larger jobs with upfront material costs, take a deposit to cover materials and bill the balance on completion. For quick call-outs, invoice in full on the day while you are still on site or same evening — small domestic jobs get paid fastest when the invoice lands immediately.

Get paid faster

Put clear payment details on the invoice, offer an easy method such as bank transfer or a card link, and send it promptly. A tidy, itemised invoice sent the same day as the work gets paid far faster than a vague one sent a week later.

Frequently asked questions

What should a handyman invoice include?

Your business details, the client and job address, an itemised split of labour, materials and any call-out fee, tax if registered, a clear total and the due date. Keep each job numbered.

Should I charge hourly or a fixed price?

Hourly suits small, unpredictable repairs; a fixed price agreed up front suits defined jobs. Whichever you use, show the detail on the invoice so the client can see what they are paying for.

Should I take a deposit?

For larger jobs with material costs up front, a deposit is sensible, with the balance due on completion. For quick call-outs, invoicing in full on the day is usually fine.
