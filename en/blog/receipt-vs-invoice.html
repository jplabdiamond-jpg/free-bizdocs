Receipt vs Invoice: What’s the Difference?

Invoicing · Free BizDoc · Oct 19, 2026

An invoice requests payment; a receipt confirms a payment was made. They look similar and often list the same items, but they do different jobs at different moments. Here’s how to tell them apart and use each correctly.

Invoice: a request for payment

You send an invoice before you’ve been paid, to tell a client what they owe and by when. It shows an invoice number, the issue and due dates, itemised charges, tax, the total due and how to pay.

Receipt: proof of payment

You issue a receipt after payment, as proof the money changed hands. It shows who paid, what for, the amount, the date and the payment method — often with a “Paid” marking. Customers keep receipts for expense claims and bookkeeping; you keep a copy for your records.

Side by side

  • Purpose — invoice: ask for payment. Receipt: confirm payment.
  • Timing — invoice: before payment. Receipt: after payment.
  • Key field — invoice: amount due + due date. Receipt: amount paid + payment method.

Do you need both?

Often, yes. For B2B work you typically send an invoice, then issue a receipt once it’s paid. For an immediate cash or card sale, a receipt alone is usually enough. If you issued an invoice first, you can create the receipt from the same details in one step — marked paid in full.

Frequently asked questions

Is a receipt the same as an invoice?

No. An invoice requests payment before it is made; a receipt confirms payment after it is made. They may list the same items but serve opposite purposes.

Do I legally need to give a receipt?

Rules vary by country and sale type, but customers often need a receipt for expense claims or tax, and many businesses issue one by default. When registered for VAT/GST, specific receipt or tax-invoice rules may apply.

Can I turn an invoice into a receipt?

Yes. Many tools let you reuse an invoice’s details to produce a receipt marked paid in full, so you do not retype the line items.
