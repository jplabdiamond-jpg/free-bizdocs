VAT vs GST vs Sales Tax: What’s the Difference?

Tax · Free BizDoc · Oct 29, 2026

VAT, GST and sales tax all tax what people buy, but they work differently — which matters when you invoice customers in different countries. Here’s the short version of how each one behaves and shows up on an invoice.

US sales tax

Sales tax is charged once, at the point of sale to the final customer, and is set by each US state (and sometimes city or county). There’s no federal sales tax. You only charge it where you have a tax obligation (“nexus”) and the item is taxable. On the invoice it’s a single line added to the subtotal.

VAT (UK and much of the world)

VAT (Value Added Tax) is charged at each stage of the supply chain, but businesses reclaim the VAT they pay, so the net effect falls on the final consumer. VAT-registered sellers show their VAT number, the rate (20% / 5% / 0% in the UK) and the VAT amount on the invoice.

GST (Australia, Canada and others)

GST (Goods and Services Tax) works much like VAT — charged along the chain with credits for businesses. Australia uses a single 10% GST and calls the document a “Tax Invoice”; Canada layers GST with provincial taxes (HST/PST/QST).

This is general information, not tax advice. Rates and rules vary and change — confirm with the relevant tax authority or your accountant.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between VAT and sales tax?

Sales tax is charged once, at the final sale, and is set by US states. VAT is charged at each stage of the supply chain with businesses reclaiming what they pay, so it’s collected gradually but ultimately borne by the consumer.

Is GST the same as VAT?

They’re very similar — both are multi-stage consumption taxes with credits for businesses. “GST” is the name used in countries like Australia and Canada; “VAT” is used in the UK and much of Europe.

Which tax do I put on my invoice?

The one for the country where the sale is taxable: US sales tax, UK VAT, or GST in Australia/Canada. A good invoice tool switches the model automatically when you choose the country.
