How to Cancel or Amend an Invoice (The Right Way)

Invoicing · Free BizDoc · Nov 8, 2026

Spotted a mistake on an invoice you already sent? The rule is simple: don’t just delete it. A sent invoice is part of your records. Here is how to correct or cancel one properly without creating a bookkeeping mess.

Before the client has paid

If the error is caught early and nothing has been paid, the cleanest fix is to cancel and reissue. Mark the original as cancelled (or issue a credit note against it), then send a corrected invoice with a new number. Tell the client briefly which invoice replaces which, so there is no confusion over what to pay.

After the client has paid

If payment has already been made and the amount was wrong, use a credit note. A credit note reverses all or part of an invoice and creates a clear paper trail — for an overcharge, credit the difference and refund it; for an undercharge, issue a new invoice for the extra amount.

Why you shouldn't just delete it

Invoice numbers should run in an unbroken sequence. Quietly deleting a numbered invoice leaves a gap that looks like something is missing and can cause problems at tax time. Cancelling or crediting keeps the sequence intact and shows exactly what happened.

Small typos vs real changes

A tiny cosmetic typo (a misspelled word) usually just needs a corrected copy. Any change to amounts, tax, dates or the client should go through a cancel-and-reissue or a credit note, because those affect the accounting.

Frequently asked questions

Can I just delete an invoice I sent by mistake?

It is better not to. A sent, numbered invoice is part of your records. Cancel it or issue a credit note and reissue a corrected invoice, so your numbering stays unbroken and the change is documented.

What is a credit note used for?

A credit note cancels or reduces an invoice that has already been issued. Use it to correct an overcharge, process a refund, or reverse an invoice sent in error, with a clear reference to the original.

Do I give the corrected invoice a new number?

Yes. Issue the corrected invoice with its own new number and reference the cancelled one, rather than reusing the old number.
