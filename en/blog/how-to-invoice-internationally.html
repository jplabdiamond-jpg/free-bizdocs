How to Invoice International Clients (Currency, Tax &amp; Payment)

Invoicing · Free BizDoc · Nov 6, 2026

Invoicing a client in another country is mostly a normal invoice — plus three decisions: which currency to bill in, how tax works across the border, and how you get paid without losing money to fees.

Pick a currency and state the rate

Agree the billing currency before the work starts. Billing in your own currency keeps your bookkeeping simple and pushes the exchange risk to the client; billing in their currency is friendlier but means the amount you receive can move. Whichever you choose, show the currency clearly (e.g. USD 1,200, not just “1,200”) so there is no confusion between dollars.

Handle tax across borders

  • Many exported services are zero-rated or outside the scope of your domestic VAT/GST — you often invoice with no tax, but you must keep evidence the client is abroad.
  • In the EU, B2B sales to a VAT-registered business usually use the reverse charge: you add no VAT and note “reverse charge” plus both VAT numbers.
  • Rules vary by country, so confirm your own before you drop the tax line.

Add the details a foreign client needs

  • Your full business name, address and (if registered) tax number
  • The client’s full name and address — useful for their own customs/tax records
  • Clear due date and accepted payment methods
  • For bank transfers: IBAN/SWIFT-BIC (or routing details) and the account currency

Choose a cheap way to get paid

A plain international wire can cost you and the client a flat fee plus a poor exchange rate. Lower-cost options include multi-currency accounts, specialist transfer services, and card or PayPal for small amounts. Say in the invoice who pays the transfer fee so the amount that lands matches what you billed.

Frequently asked questions

Do I charge tax to a client in another country?

Often no, but it depends on your country and whether you sell goods or services. Many exported services are zero-rated or outside the scope of domestic VAT/GST, while EU B2B sales use the reverse charge. Confirm your own rules and keep evidence the client is abroad.

Which currency should I invoice in?

Billing in your own currency keeps your accounts simple and avoids exchange risk; billing in the client's currency is more convenient for them. Agree it up front and always show the currency code on the invoice.

Who pays the bank transfer fee?

State it on the invoice. If you want to receive the full amount, ask the client to cover sending fees, or use a low-cost transfer service and absorb a small, predictable cost.
