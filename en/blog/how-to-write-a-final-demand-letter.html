How to Write a Final Demand Letter for an Unpaid Invoice

Getting paid · Free BizDoc · Nov 24, 2026

When polite reminders haven’t worked, a final demand letter is the last step before you escalate. Done right, it’s firm, factual, and often the thing that finally gets you paid. Here is how to write one.

When to send it

A final demand comes after you’ve already sent reminders and the invoice is clearly overdue. It signals you’re serious and that the next step is formal (collections, mediation, or a small-claims process). Don’t lead with it — its weight comes from being the last notice, not the first.

What to include

  • A clear heading: Final Demand (or Final Notice)
  • The invoice number, amount, and original due date
  • How overdue it is and any late fees that now apply
  • A firm final deadline to pay (e.g. 7 days)
  • What happens if they don’t — stated plainly, not as a threat
  • How to pay, and your contact details

Keep the tone right

Firm is not the same as angry. Keep it factual and unemotional — dates, amounts, a deadline, and the next step. A calm final demand carries more weight than an aggressive one, and it protects you if the matter does escalate, because it reads as reasonable to any third party.

After the letter

Mean what you write: if the deadline passes, follow through with the step you named. Options include a collections service, mediation, or a small-claims process, depending on the amount and your location. A final demand only works if there’s a real next step behind it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a final demand letter?

A firm, formal letter sent after reminders have failed, stating the overdue amount, a final deadline to pay, and the consequences of not paying. It's the last step before escalation.

What should a final demand letter include?

A clear 'Final Demand' heading, the invoice number, amount and original due date, how overdue it is, any late fees, a firm final deadline, what happens next, and how to pay.

Should a final demand letter be aggressive?

No. Keep it factual and calm &mdash; dates, amounts, a deadline and the next step. A measured letter carries more weight and reads as reasonable if the matter later escalates.
