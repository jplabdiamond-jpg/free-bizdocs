How to Number Invoices (A Simple System)

Invoicing · Free BizDoc · Oct 26, 2026

Every invoice needs a unique number, and in many countries they must run in a sequence with no gaps. A simple, consistent numbering system keeps you compliant, makes invoices easy to find, and looks professional to clients.

Why sequential numbers matter

Unique, sequential numbers let you (and the tax authority) track every invoice and spot anything missing. In VAT/GST systems, a continuous sequence is often a legal requirement — gaps or duplicates can raise questions.

Formats that work

  • Simple sequence: INV-0001, INV-0002, INV-0003…
  • Year prefix: 2026-001, 2026-002 (reset or continue each year).
  • Client or project code: ACME-0001 — handy if you want to group by client, as long as each number is still unique overall.

Mistakes to avoid

Don’t reuse numbers, don’t leave gaps without a record, and don’t restart in a way that creates duplicates. Pick one format and stick to it — changing systems mid-year causes confusion.

Frequently asked questions

Do invoice numbers have to be sequential?

In many countries, especially under VAT/GST, invoice numbers must be unique and run in a continuous sequence without gaps. Even where it isn’t strictly required, sequential numbering is best practice.

What is a good invoice numbering format?

A simple sequence like INV-0001, a year prefix like 2026-001, or a client code like ACME-0001 all work — as long as every number is unique and the sequence is consistent.

Can I change my invoice numbering system?

You can, but do it cleanly (for example at the start of a year) and keep a record, so you never create duplicates or unexplained gaps.
