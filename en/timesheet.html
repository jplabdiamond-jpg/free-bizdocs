A timesheet records the hours you worked so they can be approved and billed. For freelancers and contractors who charge by the hour, it is the backup for an invoice: each line is a date and task, the hours, your rate and the amount, with the total hours and total due at the bottom.

What to include on a timesheet

  • Your name or business, and the client the hours are for
  • The period (for example the week ending date) and a timesheet number
  • One line per day or task: the date and description, hours and rate
  • Total hours and the total amount at the agreed rate
  • Space for approval, if your client signs off hours before paying

From timesheet to invoice

Because a timesheet is just hours priced at a rate, it converts cleanly into an invoice. Log the week here, then switch to “Invoice” under the preview to bill the same lines with a due date and your payment details. No tax is applied to the timesheet itself — add it on the invoice if you charge it.

Frequently asked questions

What should a timesheet include?

Your name or business, the client, the period (e.g. week ending), and one line per day or task with the date, hours and rate. The PDF totals the hours and the amount due at your rate.

Does the timesheet add tax?

No. A timesheet records hours and amounts only. If you charge sales tax or VAT, convert the timesheet to an invoice — tax is applied there.

How do I turn a timesheet into an invoice?

Under the preview, switch to “Invoice”. The same lines carry over, and you can add a due date, payment terms and tax.

How do I enter hours and rate?

Each line has an Hours field and a Rate field; the amount is hours x rate. Put the date and task in the description. The total hours appear in the summary.

Is it free?

Yes — no sign-up. The free version allows 2 PDF downloads per hour from the same network.
